What each tab shows

New here, or new to betting/DFS terms in general? This page explains what each tab is for and a few terms you'll see repeated across the site.

The Challenges (what you actually do)

Slate — build your weekly DFS lineup

Pick 9 players (QB, 2 RB, 3 WR, TE, FLEX, DST) within a fixed salary cap, like a real DraftKings contest. Click a player to add them; the salary bar shows how much cap space you have left. Once a player's game starts, you can't swap in a new player from that game — but if they were already in your lineup, they stay put. Every other game on the slate stays editable until its kickoff.

Standings — how everyone's doing

Three separate rankings: season-long DFS points with your single worst week dropped, season-long DFS points with nothing dropped, and the weekly high scorer for each week. Below that, the separate Prop Bet Challenge standings — ranked by total correct picks, not accuracy percentage.

Props — pick 5 over/under bets each week

Each week you'll see a slate of player prop bets (e.g. "Bijan Robinson over/under 5.5 catches"). Pick exactly 5 and choose Over or Under on each. Once the games are played, we automatically check your picks against the real result. See "Over/Under" and "Line" in the glossary below if that's new to you.

My Lineups — look back at any past week

Pick a year, week, and participant to see exactly who was in that lineup and how each player actually scored — not just the single total number Standings shows. Defaults to your own most recent lineup, but you can look up anyone's past lineup too, since Standings already shows everyone's weekly total anyway.

Scoring and DK Points

Every score on this site uses DraftKings' own NFL scoring rules — same formula whether it's a full lineup or an implied points estimate.

Passing: 0.04 pts/yard (1 pt per 25 yards), 4 pts/TD, -1 pt/interception, +3 bonus for 300+ yards in a game.

Rushing & Receiving: 0.1 pts/yard (1 pt per 10 yards), 6 pts/TD, +3 bonus for 100+ yards in a game (rushing and receiving yards are bonused separately — a player could earn both in the same game).

Receptions: 1 pt each (full PPR), regardless of yardage.

Other offense: -1 pt per fumble lost; 2 pts for a 2-point conversion (passing, rushing, or receiving).

Defense/Special Teams (DST): 1 pt/sack, 2 pts/interception, 2 pts/fumble recovery, 6 pts for any defensive or special-teams touchdown, 2 pts/safety, plus a tiered bonus based on points allowed that game (fewer points allowed means more bonus points, with the bonus turning negative once the defense allows a lot of points).

DraftKings occasionally adjusts these numbers — this reflects their standard rules as of when this was written; their own rules page is the final word if something here ever looks off.

Reference & Research (data to help you decide)

History — past weeks' salaries and results

Browse any past week's slate alongside what actually happened — salary, projected points, and actual DK points scored. Switch the week dropdown to "Season" to see full-season totals per player instead of one week at a time.

Weather — game conditions

Temperature, wind, and conditions for each game. A "Forecast" badge means the game hasn't been played yet and conditions could still change; "Final" means the game's over and this is what actually happened.

Game Info — the broader game context

Roof type, playing surface, attendance, and the Vegas point spread/total for each game — useful context beyond just weather (e.g. an indoor dome game won't be affected by wind at all).

Schedule — the full season, organized by day

Every week's games grouped by which day they're played (Thursday, Sunday, Monday, and the occasional other day) rather than one long flat list.

Fantasy Pts Against — which defenses are weak matchups

Shows how many fantasy points each team has given up to a given position (QB/RB/WR/TE) this season. A high number means that defense has been generous to that position — a potentially good matchup for a player facing them.

Team Points — scoring by team

Points scored and allowed by each team, week by week, or switch to "Season" for their average per game across the whole year.

Best Matchups — this week's most exploitable matchups

Cross-references this week's slate against each player's specific opponent's recent history of points allowed to that position, plus the player's own recent scoring — both averaged over their last 10 games (crossing into last season if needed early in a new one). Sorted so the best combination of "weak opponent + productive player" shows up first. Only shows players actually on their team's depth chart this week.

Depth Charts — who's actually playing, and in what order

Each team's 1st/2nd/3rd string at QB, RB, WR, and TE. Useful for spotting backups who might see extra usage, or confirming a player is even in the mix at all.

Implied Player Points — what the betting market expects

Converts this week's prop betting lines into an estimated fantasy point total per player, using DraftKings scoring rates. Different from a traditional projection — it's derived purely from what the market is pricing in. Two real gaps: doesn't include anytime-touchdown props (priced as odds, not a line) or combined passing-and-rushing-yards props (mixes two different scoring rates that can't be safely separated), so these totals run a bit low for touchdown-heavy players.

Implied Team Points — how many points each team is expected to score

A standard formula derived from the game's spread and total: implied total = (O/U ÷ 2) − (spread ÷ 2). A team favored by more points gets a higher implied total; both teams' numbers always add up to the game's O/U. Useful for spotting which games are expected to be high-scoring shootouts versus defensive grind-outs, at a glance.

Terms you'll see around the site

Over/Under (and "the line")

A prop bet's "line" is a number set for a specific stat (like passing yards). Betting the Over means you think the real result will be higher than that number; Under means you think it'll be lower. E.g. "Over 5.5 catches" wins if the player catches 6 or more.

Odds (+150, -120, etc.)

American odds format. A negative number (like -120) means you'd need to bet $120 to win $100 — that side is considered more likely. A positive number (like +150) means a $100 bet would win $150 — that side is considered less likely, but pays out more if it hits.

Salary cap, Proj Pts, and Value

In DFS, every player has a "salary" and you're building a 9-player lineup under a fixed total budget (the salary cap) — same idea as a real DraftKings contest. "Proj Pts" is a projection of how many points they'll likely score; "Value" is projected points per $1,000 of salary, a quick way to spot good bang-for-your-buck picks.

Merging downloaded data across tabs

Most downloads already share the same year, week, and team columns, so combining two of them in Excel/Sheets is a direct match on those columns (e.g. a VLOOKUP/XLOOKUP on team). Weather and Game Info are the exception — they're stored one row per game rather than per team. Use the "Download for Merging with History" button on those two pages instead of their regular download to get a per-team version that matches everyone else's format.

What each tab shows

New to DFS or betting terms? Here's what each tab is for, plus a glossary of terms used around the site.

The Challenges

Slate: build your weekly DFS lineup

Pick 9 players (QB, 2 RB, 3 WR, TE, FLEX, DST) under the salary cap, like a DraftKings contest. Click a player to add them; the salary bar shows how much cap you have left. Once a game starts, you can't add a new player from it, but players already in your lineup stay put. Every other game stays editable until its kickoff.

To narrow the table, filter by position, search by name, or use the checkboxes: "Hide locked" hides players whose game has started, and "Hide 2nd string" / "Hide 3rd string" hide backups. Filters stack. Players with no depth chart entry show a dash in the String column and are never hidden by the string filters.

Standings: how everyone's doing

Three DFS rankings: season points with your worst week dropped, season points with nothing dropped, and each week's high scorer. Below that are the Prop Bet Challenge standings, ranked by total correct picks (not accuracy percentage).

Props: pick 5 over/under bets each week

Each week has a list of player props (e.g. "Bijan Robinson over/under 5.5 catches"). Pick exactly 5 and choose Over or Under on each. Picks are graded automatically against the real stats after the games. New to this? See "Over/Under" in the glossary below.

Props lock per game, like the Slate. Once a player's game kicks off, the prop shows a LOCKED badge and you can't pick it, switch Over/Under, or swap it for another prop. Props in games that haven't started stay editable.

My Props: your past picks

Pick a year, week, and participant to see each pick, its line, what the player did, and whether it hit. Defaults to your most recent week.

My Lineups: your past lineups

Pick a year, week, and participant to see who was in that lineup and what each player scored. Defaults to your most recent lineup, and you can look up anyone's.

Scoring and DK Points

All scores use DraftKings NFL scoring, for both lineups and implied points.

Passing: 0.04 pts/yard (1 pt per 25 yards), 4 pts/TD, -1 pt/interception, +3 bonus for 300+ yards in a game.

Rushing & Receiving: 0.1 pts/yard (1 pt per 10 yards), 6 pts/TD, +3 bonus for 100+ yards in a game. Rushing and receiving bonuses are separate, so a player can earn both in one game.

Receptions: 1 pt each (full PPR), regardless of yardage.

Other offense: -1 pt per fumble lost; 2 pts for a 2-point conversion (passing, rushing, or receiving).

Defense/Special Teams (DST): 1 pt/sack, 2 pts/interception, 2 pts/fumble recovery, 6 pts for any defensive or special-teams touchdown, 2 pts/safety, plus a tiered bonus based on points allowed that game. Fewer points allowed means a bigger bonus, and it turns negative when the defense gives up a lot.

DraftKings sometimes changes these. If something looks off, their rules page is the final word.

Reference & Research

History: past salaries and results

Any past week's salaries, projected points, and actual DK points. Set the week dropdown to "Season" for full-season totals per player.

Game Overview: every game on one screen

This week's spread, total, weather, roof type, and implied team points for each game, in one table. Always shows the current week, since betting lines only exist for upcoming games.

Weather: game conditions

Temperature, wind, and conditions for each game. "Forecast" means the game hasn't been played and conditions could change. "Final" means the game is over and shows what happened.

Game Info: more game context

Roof type, playing surface, attendance, and the Vegas spread and total for each game. For example, wind won't affect a dome game.

Schedule: the full season by day

Each week's games grouped by day (Thursday, Sunday, Monday, and the occasional other day).

Fantasy Pts Against: weak defenses by position

Fantasy points each team has allowed to QBs, RBs, WRs, and TEs this season. A high number means that defense has been generous to the position, which can make it a good matchup.

Usage: a player's share of the offense

Season-to-date target share and touch share: a player's targets (or rush attempts plus catches) as a percentage of the team's total. A high target share means a player is getting looks even if the points haven't followed yet.

Shares are out of the team's season total. Some sites use the same name for snap share; we don't have snap counts.

Team Points: scoring by team

Points scored and allowed by each team, week by week. Switch to "Season" for per-game averages.

Best Matchups: this week's best spots

Compares each player on this week's slate against what their opponent has allowed to that position, plus the player's own recent scoring. Both use the last 10 games (reaching back into last season early in the year). Weak opponent plus productive player sorts to the top. Only players on their team's depth chart this week are shown.

Depth Charts: who's playing, in what order

Each team's 1st, 2nd, and 3rd string at QB, RB, WR, and TE. Handy for spotting backups who might get extra work, or checking that a player is in the mix.

Implied Player Points: what betting lines expect

Turns this week's player prop lines into estimated fantasy points using DraftKings scoring. Anytime TD props and combined passing-plus-rushing yards props aren't included, so totals run a bit low for touchdown-heavy players.

Implied Team Points: expected points per team

Calculated from each game's spread and total: implied total = (O/U ÷ 2) − (spread ÷ 2). Bigger favorites get higher totals, and both teams always add up to the game's O/U. Useful for spotting likely shootouts and low-scoring games.

Terms you'll see around the site

Over/Under (and "the line")

A prop's "line" is a number for a specific stat, like passing yards. Over means you think the result will be higher; Under means lower. For example, "Over 5.5 catches" wins if the player catches 6 or more.

Odds (+150, -120, etc.)

American odds. A negative number (like -120) means you bet $120 to win $100, and that side is more likely. A positive number (like +150) means a $100 bet wins $150; that side is less likely but pays more.

Salary cap, Proj Pts, and Value

Each player has a salary, and your 9-player lineup has to fit under the salary cap, like a DraftKings contest. "Proj Pts" is projected points. "Value" is projected points per $1,000 of salary, a quick way to find bargains.

Downloading data

Every tab has CSV downloads with the same labels:

  • This Week / This Season / This Team: what's on screen, for the year, week, position, or team you've selected.
  • All Data: every year and week we have. Use this for season-long analysis.
  • Merge-Friendly: This Week / Merge-Friendly: All Data: Weather and Game Info only. See the next section.
Merging downloaded data across tabs

Every download starts with the same key columns. What you join on depends on what one row represents:

One row is…Join onTabs
a player in a week year + week + name_normalized Slate, History, Best Matchups, Implied Player Points, Props, My Props, My Lineups, a player's career page
a player in a season year + name_normalized Usage
a team in a week year + week + team Team Points, Implied Team Points, Depth Charts, Weather and Game Info (merge-friendly button only)
a team in a season year + team (+ position) Fantasy Pts Against
a single game year + week + home_team/away_team Weather, Game Info, Game Overview, Schedule
a participant in a week year + week + submitter Standings

Join players on name_normalized. Sources spell names differently ("A.J. Brown" vs "AJ Brown", "Patrick Mahomes II" vs "Patrick Mahomes"), so matching on name will miss rows. name_normalized is lowercase with punctuation removed, and it's on every player-level download.

Game-level files. Weather, Game Info, Game Overview and Schedule have one row per game, with separate home_team and away_team columns. VLOOKUP and XLOOKUP can't match a team against either column. Weather and Game Info have Merge-Friendly buttons that split each game into two rows (one per team) with a plain team column, for one week or all weeks. Use those when combining either tab with anything else.

In the merge-friendly Weather file, score is that row's team's score and opponent_score is the other team's, in place of home_score/away_score.

Joining on more than one column. VLOOKUP and XLOOKUP match on one column only. Add a helper column in both sheets first, e.g. =A2&"-"&B2&"-"&C2 to combine year, week and name_normalized, then look up on that.
